01 · SOFTNEST

Replacing NestFab for ply nesting.

~5%Average material saving versus NestFab
8.52%Documented case: 256 placements, 0 unplaced
300+DXF templates tested on two real jobs
<1 yearPayback from the license alone, ~8 h of development

Problem

Carbon-fiber ply templates were nested on the prepreg roll with NestFab, an annual license used for one narrow job. Prepreg is expensive, so every percent of roll length matters.

How I Tackled It

  • Used a published strip-packing solver (Sparrow, via spyrrow) instead of a home-made heuristic.
  • Added what production needs: DXF import, quantities, fixed/orthogonal/free rotation per template.
  • Rebuilt the CAD geometry and re-checked collisions with Shapely, independently of the solver.
  • Exported cutter DXF plus PDF and CSV reports.

Result

  • ~5% less material than NestFab on average.
  • No recurring license for this job.
  • Verified, not yet in production; the 15% target of one case was not reached.

02 · Quad-Dominant Mesher

Learning from ANSA, then meshing with open-source tools.

100% vs 98.5%Quads on a 29-coupon benchmark, versus ANSA
97.2% vs 98.2%Quads on a real 83-skin part, versus ANSA
≤0.4%Quads outside quality limits on two real parts
73 → 30Benchmark penalty after one night of tuning

Problem

Composite surface meshes were made entirely in ANSA, even for narrow jobs. STEP exports split smooth surfaces into tiny faces and fillet strips, and a naive mesher distorts elements exactly there.

How I Tackled It

  • Imported ANSA meshes and scored them: a structured grid almost everywhere (45 irregular nodes in 97,000).
  • Grouped faces into zones bounded only by sharp edges.
  • Flattened each zone, laid a structured grid, joined it to the border with one row chosen by dynamic programming, then projected back onto the exact CAD.
  • Froze my first hand-written engine as a reference and moved to Gmsh; tuned on a 7-case benchmark.

Result

  • Ahead of ANSA on coupons, close on real parts.
  • Still more elements and higher skew than ANSA.
  • Goal: replace ANSA for specific jobs, not all meshing.

03 · DRAFTAI

From a part surface to carbon mold pieces in minutes.

Problem

Splitting a mold meant analyzing pull directions, choosing parting lines and the number of pieces, then drawing each one: a working day or more per part.

How I Tackled It

  • Meshed the surface with Gmsh and grouped normals by feasible pull direction, with a 10° minimum draft.
  • Searched the main direction that needs the fewest parting planes.
  • Built 60 mm planar flanges and 3-axis-millable resin patterns with OpenCascade.
  • Wrote results back into CATIA as native, colored extracts, never saving automatically.

Result

  • Correct pull directions on 3–4 test parts versus manual analysis.
  • Minutes of computation plus mandatory review.
  • Complex geometry is the open limit; not yet used on a job.

04 · CFRP Void Measurement

A photograph in, traceable void dimensions out.

Problem

Voids on carbon specimens were judged by eye, with no record. A phone photo has perspective and no scale, so counting pixels is not a measurement.

How I Tackled It

  • Rectified the specimen with a projective homography, scaled by its known width.
  • Proposed dark enclosed cavities with a multi-scale OpenCV detector.
  • Required a person to accept, redraw or reject every proposal before measuring.
  • Reported Feret, area and an expanded uncertainty (k = 2) for each void.

Result

  • Working review workflow; ~20 test images.
  • Target resolution 0.1 mm.
  • Next: calibration for any specimen width and tests on company specimens.

05 · MBB Studio

The smallest raw-stock block, in about twenty seconds.

Problem

Raw-stock size was estimated in CATIA: a few minutes per part and not precise, so stock was often oversized.

How I Tackled It

  • Reduced each STEP or STL part to its convex hull.
  • Started from principal axes, then ran an evolutionary orientation search with local refinement.
  • Verified full containment, then added the machining offset.

Result

  • ~20 s per part instead of 3–5 min.
  • ~20 parts checked, all fully contained.
  • Heuristic: no proof of the global minimum yet.
